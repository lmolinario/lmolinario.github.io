FAIR-Lab: measuring when forensic AI stops being reliable.

Forensic AI Robustness Lab is a reproducible evaluation framework for studying how AI-assisted forensic image classification behaves when evidence departs from clean benchmark conditions.

Frozen dataset
1,500

Manually reviewed images divided into weapon, non-weapon and OOD groups.

Binary subset
1,000

Weapon/non-weapon images used for clean, adversarial and anti-forensic robustness experiments.

Evaluation model
4 modes

Clean · adversarial · anti-forensic · out-of-distribution.

Research objective

Operational robustness

The objective is not simply to maximize classification accuracy. It is to determine whether AI-assisted forensic workflows remain useful and defensible when image evidence is perturbed, manipulated, source-shifted or otherwise unlike the conditions under which a model performs best.

Dataset design

Traceable evaluation inputs

The frozen dataset contains 500 weapon images, 500 non-weapon images and 500 OOD images. Selection is based on explicit review criteria, with manifests, hashes and traceability records supporting repeatable evaluation.

Evaluation conditions

The framework treats robustness as a family of state changes rather than one adversarial-attack score.

Clean baseline

Reference performance on the unmodified evaluation set.

Adversarial

Controlled perturbations used to measure model brittleness under intentional attack.

Anti-forensic

Recompression, blur, resize, contrast and colour changes used to model practical evidence transformations.

OOD

Borderline or source-shifted inputs used to observe false assurance beyond the intended distribution.

Explainability

Visual attribution used to inspect selected failure cases and support human interpretation.

Models and analysis

Transparent proxy layer

EfficientNet-B0, ResNet18 and a CLIP visual encoder provide controlled baselines for robustness measurement, comparison and explainability.

EfficientNet-B0ResNet18CLIPXAI

Black-box evaluation

Forensic-oriented commercial workflows are treated as black boxes where internal model details are unavailable, keeping the focus on observable behaviour and operational consequences.

Outputs used to support conclusions

Results are documented through measurable artifacts rather than descriptive claims alone.

Robustness metrics

Clean FNR, adversarial FNR, anti-forensic FNR, overall FPR and OOD weapon-rate summaries.

Comparative evidence

Confusion matrices, condition-level comparisons and failure-case review.

Reproducibility

Frozen manifests, hashes, traceable subsets and documented evaluation conditions.

Research position: in evidence-sensitive workflows, a model that performs well on clean data can still be operationally unsafe if its failure modes are opaque, easy to trigger or difficult to detect.
